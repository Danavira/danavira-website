I was invited to this interview three weeks after applying, which I guess is relatively normal in this day and age. Without revealing too much, the role I applied for was an intersection between data and DevOps, so it aligned quite well with my background and what I was looking for.

### Preparation

I prepared for the interview by reviewing past notes about my work experience; what I did, the extent of my involvement in building things, and the lessons I learned. I also went through a little bit of theory to refresh, and dove deep into the company and its products. I went into the interview pretty confident since I felt prepared, but what I was in fact not prepared for were the questions that actually came.

### The Questions

In short, I was asked both technical and behavioral questions. The interview started with the interviewer introducing a bit about the company and asking me questions about my career so far.

Then came technical questions about networking, my admitted weakest point. Not being able to answer those correctly was when the panic started. Thankfully he kindly decided to not bully me and moved on. 

Some of the other questions were completely unexpected:

- _"What was the best 6 months of your career so far?"_ and _"What was the worst 6 months of your career so far?"_ . These caught me off guard, though I believe I gave appropriate answers that gave us both a laugh.
- _"What is your favorite database?"_ . This was something I had never thought about, so it threw me off. I said ClickHouse because of its compression, though to be honest, I knew very little about what actually makes ClickHouse's compression so efficient.
- **Kubernetes:** A few questions came up about Kubernetes as well, and I believe I answered them quite well based on the interviewer's reaction and feedback.

### The Turning Point

I was also given the opportunity to explain the data flow of the pipelines I had built. He responded with something along the lines of, _"If an engineer under me came up to me with that pipeline architecture, I would be tremendously disappointed,"_ which essentially paralyzed my brain right there on the spot.

He went on to explain why, and I understood his reasoning. I mentioned that back then, I had little say in the design of the pipeline, and looking back, there are many things I would change. Ultimately, I think he was able to accept that.

Throughout the interview, he was very patient and brushed off my lack of knowledge as a lack of experience. While I appreciated that, it probably meant I wasn't experienced enough for the role. At the very end, he asked me, _"So, do you still want this job?"_ And me, being the nervous idiot that I was and always am, said, _"Well, do you want to hire me?"_...... The moment it came out of my mouth, I knew I had dug my grave.

### Takeaway

Overall, I am really thankful for the interview, not just for the opportunity to interview for the role, but for having had that conversation with the interviewer. He was very knowledgeable about the internals of stacks and systems, taught me that I still have a long way to go, and gave really good, sincere advice on what I should do as a young engineer. I learned more from that interview than I thought I would, and it was mostly thanks to him.

If by some miracle you (the interviewer) find this post and are reading this, I'd just like to say: thanks.
